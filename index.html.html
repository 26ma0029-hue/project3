01 — About the Archive

Reading regional history
through spatial ruins.

A student-led Digital Humanities spatial project integrating architectural field surveys, historical cartography, mining archives, and visual provenance.

The forested plateaus, river valleys, and mineral basins of Jharkhand preserve a fragile, layered record of historical and architectural transition. Ancient and medieval stone fortresses of Chero, Nagvanshi, and Ramgarh dynasties sit alongside deserted colonial-era zamindari rajbaris, abandoned terminal railway stations, and toxic, continuously burning colliery landscapes in the Damodar Valley.

These decaying physical structures serve as primary artifacts of indigenous tribal sovereignty, colonial resource extraction, and rapid industrial displacement that often remain under-documented in mainstream archival narratives. Many locations—such as the burning pits of Jharia or ruined outposts like Palasthali and Teliagarhi—face ongoing threats from weathering, commercial mining expansion, and subsidence.

By implementing a rigorous Digital Humanities framework, this project establishes a verifiable, source-transparent digital record. Each site is documented with its geographic locality, architectural condition, image attribution, and independent historical references—safeguarding cultural memory from physical and digital disappearance.

“A ruin is not simply a structure that has disappeared from utility; it is an active archive that speaks continuously through material decay, landscape memory, and community history.”
Archive at a Glance

Teliagarhi Fort

Karamtola, near Sahibganj

Teliagarhi Fort, located in the Sahibganj district of Jharkhand, is believed to be the area's earliest monument, built during Shah Jahan's reign by a local Teli Zamindar who converted to Islam. Historically designated as the 'Gateway to Bengal' (Dwar-e-Bangal), the citadel occupied a vital strategic pass between the Rajmahal Hills and the Ganges River, witnessing decisive military encounters involving Sher Shah Suri, Mughal forces, and regional rulers.

⌖ Sahibganj, Jharkhand Verified Visual Record

Rajmahal Trap Stone Quarries

Rajmahal Hills (Sahibganj & Pakur border)

The Rajmahal Trap stone quarries are intensive basalt and black trap-rock mining sites located across the Sahibganj and Pakur districts of northeastern Jharkhand. Formed during ancient volcanic eruptions in the Jurassic period, the dense igneous rock has been quarried for over a century for railway ballast, road metal, and building foundations, leaving behind large flooded pits and scarred ridges.

Nala Block (near West Bengal border)

Palasthali Railway Station (station code: PSME) is an abandoned terminal station in the Nala block of Jamtara district, Jharkhand, near the West Bengal border. Jharia Coalfield

Jharia Mining Belt

Jharia Coalfield in Dhanbad district, Jharkhand, contains India's largest and most persistent underground coal fires, burning continuously since 1916 due to unscientific colonial extraction, exposed seams, and spontaneous combustion. Large tracts of land, residential neighborhoods, and railway alignments have been evacuated and declared subsidence zones, leaving an eerie landscape of smoking fissures and derelict infrastructure.

Palamu Fort

Betla National Park / Auranga River bank

Palamu Fort comprises two monumental ruined fortresses (Purana Qila and Naya Qila) situated deep within the Betla sal forests along the Auranga River in Latehar district (historically Palamu). Built in the 17th century by tribal Chero kings, notably Raja Medini Rai and Raja Pratap Rai, the forts fell into abandonment following Mughal sieges under Daud Khan in 1660, British military demolition following the 1857 revolt, and dense jungle encroachment.

09 Colonial & Heritage Settlements

McCluskieganj: The 'Mini London' of India

Khelari Block

Founded in 1933 by Ernest Timothy McCluskie through the Colonisation Society of India, this quiet town in Ranchi district was built as a promised land and homeland for the Anglo-Indian community on 10,000 acres leased from the Maharaja of Ratu. Known colloquially as 'Mini London', it once had over 300 Anglo-Indian families. Following post-independence emigration to Australia, the UK, and Canada, dozens of European-style bungalows were left deserted, evoking the aura of an unfulfilled colonial dream.

Pithoria Fort

Pithoria, Kanke Block

Pithoria Fort, located approximately 20 km north of Ranchi, was the fortified palace of King Jagatpal Singh. During the Indian Rebellion of 1857, Jagatpal Singh allied with the British Army instead of supporting local revolutionaries led by Thakur Vishwanath Shahdeo. Local folklore recounts that the captured rebel leader cursed the fort before his execution, prophesying it would crumble under repeated lightning strikes, and the decaying stone ruins show extensive lightning scarring and structural collapse.

Padma Fort

Padma Block

The Padma Fort (also known as Padma Palace or Hawa Mahal) is located in Padma, near Hazaribagh in Jharkhand. Built in the late 19th and early 20th century as the last capital seat of the Rajas of Ramgarh (after Ichak was deserted), it incorporates neoclassical colonnades, arched entrance gates, and vast courtyards. Shahpur Fort

Opposite Daltonganj / Medininagar, North Koel River

Shahpur Fort is situated on a rocky ridge on the western bank of the North Koel River, directly opposite Medininagar (Daltonganj) in Palamu district. Built around 1772 by Raja Gopal Rai, a descendant of legendary Chero king Medini Rai during succession conflicts, the stone citadel was intended as a defensive redoubt and palace. Navratangarh Fort

Sisai Block (Doisagarh)

Navratangarh Fort (also called Doisagarh) is situated in Sisai block of Gumla district. Gibraltar House

Kanhari Hills (Canary Hills)

Gibraltar House was a historic colonial mansion built approximately 150 years ago at Kanhari (Canary) Hills in Hazaribagh town by S. C. Mallick, a judge of the Calcutta High Court. Designed as a scenic hilltop retreat during the British Raj, the building had fallen into neglect. Jharia Mining Belt, BCCL Lodna Area

Gajlitand Colliery

Katras Area, BCCL Area IV

Gajlitand Colliery is an underground coal mine located in the Katras area of Dhanbad, operated under Bharat Coking Coal Limited (BCCL). It was the site of the catastrophic 26 September 1995 mine disaster when the swollen Katri River breached an embankment following heavy monsoon rainfall, flooding the mine and drowning 64 miners. The pithead stands as an abandoned disaster memorial.

03 — Archival Methodology & Transparency

Academic Rigour &
Source Provenance

This archive is an academic Digital Humanities project developed by postgraduate students. Archival integrity requires full methodological disclosure of provenance and auditing decisions.

Digital Humanities projects must not replicate the superficial presentation of web image aggregators. Every entry in this archive is governed by four core methodological standards established during our 2026–2027 audit:

1. The Dual-Source Principle

An image source and a historical research source serve fundamentally distinct scholarly functions. In this archive, research papers, government reports, and historical treatises are strictly distinguished from the original platforms, photographers, or institutional repositories from which visual representations originate. A research paper is never cited as an image source unless it demonstrably contains the original photograph.

2. Visual Mismatch & Placeholders

Where previous documentation conflated distant geographic locations—such as illustrating Dharmabandh Coalfield in Dhanbad with a photograph of Talcher in Odisha—the mismatched image has been removed. Rather than fabricating visual records, we display an explicit academic placeholder indicating that a site-specific field survey is required.

3. Transparent Licensing & Fair Dealing

Photographs included in this repository are credited to their original creators, institutional repositories, or Wikimedia contributors under Creative Commons licenses (CC BY-SA 4.0, CC BY 3.0), Public Domain (US NARA DOCUMERICA), or educational Fair Dealing principles. Where licensing cannot be independently verified, entries are transparently marked as "Unverified / Editorial review".

Geographical Audit & Interstate Boundary Documentation

Our geographical audit confirmed 27 records strictly located within the 24 districts of Jharkhand, alongside 1 explicitly documented interstate record (Salanpur Coal Mines in Paschim Bardhaman, West Bengal). Salanpur is situated directly on the interstate border and belongs to the contiguous Damodar Valley coal extraction belt operated by Eastern Coalfields Limited (ECL). It is included with a clear Interstate Border Site indicator to prevent geographic confusion.

04 — Metadata Framework

Structured Spatial
Heritage Archiving

A reusable Dublin Core-aligned metadata schema ensures that visual documentation, geography, and archival provenance can be queried systematically.

Structured Information in Digital Humanities: Raw historical anecdotes and uncurated web photography lack academic utility without consistent metadata. By standardizing each record into six verifiable dimensions, our dataset allows researchers to perform cross-district comparative studies, industrial transition analyses, and spatial condition tracking.

This standardized schema also facilitates future integration into Geographic Information Systems (GIS), LOD (Linked Open Data) ontologies, and collaborative digital gazetteers for eastern India's vernacular heritage.
